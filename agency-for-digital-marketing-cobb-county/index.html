Scope, timing and terms to understand

Here are useful facts to discuss before work begins:

  • Coverage: Services can include search, content, social media, video, branding, website work and advertising.
  • Timing: The schedule depends on the services, project size and what information or materials are ready. Ask for the proposed stages and timing rather than assuming a standard turnaround.
  • Cost factors: The amount and type of work, the number of services and the needs of a campaign can all affect cost. Request a written scope so you can see what the proposed work covers. No specific price applies to every project.

A few plain-English definitions can make agency conversations easier. SEO is short for search engine optimization, work focused on helping a website appear in relevant search results. Content marketing uses useful or relevant content to support a business’s marketing. Website optimization means improving parts of a website so it better serves its purpose. Lead generation is the work of encouraging and capturing potential customer inquiries.

Compare proposals by fit, not buzzwords

For an agency for digital marketing Woodstock business owners are considering, ask what the agency recommends and why. Check whether the proposal names the services, explains who provides needed materials and describes how the work will be reviewed. You should be able to tell what is included and what would need a separate discussion.

Compare providers on how well they understand your goals, too. Ask how SEO, website changes and advertising might relate to the outcome you want. A list of familiar terms is less useful than a plan that explains the role of each service in plain language.
